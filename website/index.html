Cancer Regulatory RNA Atlas

An evidence-based resource for exploring noncoding RNA regulation in human cancer.

Independent project · v0.1 prototype · No institutional affiliation or endorsement · Not medical information

About this atlas

Research on regulatory noncoding RNAs in cancer is scattered across databases that each cover one RNA class and each use their own notion of evidence. This atlas organizes published findings under a single evidence framework, so the strength of support for every record is visible and comparable.

RNA classes covered

Read this before using the numbers above.

Counts reflect how much has been curated, not how common anything is in biology. The records were chosen because they are well documented and traceable, which biases them toward highly cited work and well studied cancer types. An RNA absent from this atlas is not evidence that it is unstudied.

Evidence classes in v0.1 were assigned from PubMed abstracts rather than full texts. Where an abstract asserts a target without naming the assay, the weaker class is recorded — so some records understate the evidence that exists in the paper.

This atlas contains no original discoveries. Every record restates something already published and links back to its source. The only contribution is organization and explicit evidence labelling.

Questions it is built to help answer

  • Which regulatory RNAs are associated with which cancer types?
  • Which genes do they regulate, and by what reported mechanism?
  • Which relationships have direct experimental support, and which rest on prediction or correlation?
  • Which relationships recur across more than one cancer type?
  • Where is the evidence thin — which RNA classes and cancer types are under-covered?

Methodology, sources and limitations

The full documents live in the repository under documentation/. This is a summary of how records were made and what they can and cannot support.

How records were curated

Records were curated by hand from two official sources: PubMed via the NCBI E-utilities for publication metadata and abstracts, and NCBI Gene for stable identifiers. Nothing was entered from memory. Where a candidate identifier did not resolve to the expected paper, the candidate was discarded rather than corrected by guesswork; the discarded candidates are recorded in the repository.

Evidence classification rules

Direct experimental
The publication reports a physical or direct interaction test — a reporter assay with binding-site mutation, RNA pulldown, RIP, CLIP or equivalent.
Experimental functional
The RNA was perturbed experimentally and the target or phenotype changed, but no direct-binding evidence is recorded in the source consulted.
Computational prediction
Inferred by algorithm, sequence complementarity or statistical association only. Never recorded as experimentally validated.
Literature association
Expression correlation, clinical or prognostic association, or genomic alteration, with no causal test.

The class reflects what the consulted source actually states. Where a source asserts a target relationship without naming the assay, the weaker class is recorded and the ambiguity noted on the record.

Missing information

An empty field means nothing was recorded. It never means the source tested and found no effect. This atlas holds no negative results, because the literature largely does not publish them.

Retraction screening

Parts of the noncoding-RNA literature, particularly lncRNA sponge papers from roughly 2016–2020, have been affected by retractions and paper-mill output. Every candidate was checked for PubMed publication-type flags. Papers flagged as retracted were excluded. This used PubMed flags only; Retraction Watch has not yet been checked, and PubMed flags can lag a publisher's notice.

Known limitations

  • Provisional evidence classes. v0.1 classes come from abstracts, not full texts.
  • Small, non-random sample. Counts measure curation effort, not biological frequency.
  • Thin enhancer RNA coverage. No curated database catalogues eRNA–target interactions the way miRTarBase does for miRNAs, so eRNA records come from individual papers and several eRNAs have no stable identifier anywhere. This is a gap in the field.
  • miRNA over-representation. miRNA databases are the most accessible, which pulls any atlas toward miRNAs unless it is corrected for deliberately.
  • Correlation is not causation. A literature association says nothing about mechanism or direction of causation.
  • No inferred mechanism. Interactions are not chained into pathways that no source states.
  • Not clinical information. This is a literature index for research purposes.

Source status

Resources reached during curation and used for records: PubMed / E-utilities, NCBI Gene, and Europe PMC (partially, before it began rate limiting). Resources named in the project plan but not yet accessed: miRTarBase, ENCORI / starBase, RNAcentral, LncBook, LncRNADisease and GEO. Their licence terms must be read before any record is imported from them.
